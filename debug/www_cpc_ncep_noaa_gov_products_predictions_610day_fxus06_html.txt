Prognostic Discussion for 6 to 10 and 8 to 14 day outlooks 
NWS Climate Prediction Center College Park, MD 
300 PM EST Thu November 12 2026 
 
6-10 DAY OUTLOOK FOR NOV 18 - 22 2026 
 
Dynamical ensemble guidance from the GEFS, ECENS, and CMCE, are in good  
agreement on the 5 day  mean <a href="/products/predictions/610day/glossary.html#500-hPa">500-hPa</a> height pattern during the 6 to 10 day  
period. Positive <a href="/products/predictions/610day/glossary.html#500-hPa">500-hPa</a> height <a href="/products/predictions/610day/glossary.html#anomalies">anomalies</a> are favored from Alaska across much  
of Canada into the eastern contiguous U.S. (CONUS). Negative <a href="/products/predictions/610day/glossary.html#500-hPa">500-hPa</a> height  
<a href="/products/predictions/610day/glossary.html#anomalies">anomalies</a> are forecast for the western <a href="/products/predictions/610day/glossary.html#CONUS">CONUS</a> and western Alaska into the Bering  
Sea. Daily model guidance indicates a mid-level low over the West Coast early  
in the period progressing inland with another <a href="/products/predictions/610day/glossary.html#trough">trough</a> following behind, although  
this area of negative <a href="/products/predictions/610day/glossary.html#anomalies">anomalies</a> is unlikely to make it to the West Coast by day  
10. The first area of mid-level low pressure is forecast to eject into the  
Plains by days 9 and 10. In Alaska, subtle differences in the mid-level height  
pattern are forecast by the GEFS, favoring stronger ridging, and the ECENS,  
favoring weaker ridging, which will impact the precipitation forecasts over the  
eastern portions of the state. 
 
Above normal temperatures are forecast across most of the <a href="/products/predictions/610day/glossary.html#CONUS">CONUS</a> as a result of  
mid-level high pressure across much of the eastern and northwestern <a href="/products/predictions/610day/glossary.html#CONUS">CONUS</a>. The  
highest probabilities for above normal temperatures, greater than 80%, are over  
the Great Lakes region. Odds are reduced across portions of the Southeast and  
Gulf Coast with lower height <a href="/products/predictions/610day/glossary.html#anomalies">anomalies</a>. Over the West, below normal  
temperatures are favored with mid-level low pressure moving across the region  
during the 6-10 day period. Warming temperatures are favored by the end of the  
period and into the week-2 period, particularly across California and Oregon.  
In Alaska, above normal temperatures are likely over most of the state with the  
highest degree of confidence over the eastern Mainland. Meanwhile, in Hawaii,  
above normal temperatures are favored over the eastern islands with near normal  
favored for Oahu and Kauai.  
 
Above normal precipitation is favored from Oregon and California through much  
of the Rockies and into the Plains and Mississippi valley associated with  
mid-level low pressure progressing across the southern tier and cyclogenesis in  
the lee of the Rockies. There is some uncertainty with the track of any area of  
low pressure that may form. The GEFS takes the low towards the Great Lakes  
while the ECENS tracks the low into the Middle-Mississippi Valley. A more  
southern route would favor drier conditions over the Upper Mississippi Valley.  
Below normal precipitation is favored over the northeastern <a href="/products/predictions/610day/glossary.html#CONUS">CONUS</a> ahead of any  
precipitation from the eastward moving system. Below normal is also favored for  
portions of Washington state closer to mid-level ridging. In Alaska, below  
normal precipitation is favored for Southeast Alaska, while above normal is  
generally favored over much of the Mainland. In Hawaii, below normal  
precipitation is favored for the island chain, consistent with consolidation  
tools and the winter <a href="/products/predictions/610day/glossary.html#El Nino">El Nino</a> signal. 
 
The official 6-10 day <a href="/products/predictions/610day/glossary.html#500-hPa">500-hPa</a> height <a href="/products/predictions/610day/glossary.html#blend">blend</a> consists of 10% of Today's 0z <a href="/products/predictions/610day/glossary.html#GFS">GFS</a>  
Ensemble Mean centered on Day 8, 25% of Today's 6z <a href="/products/predictions/610day/glossary.html#GFS">GFS</a> Ensemble Mean centered  
on Day 8, 40% of Today's 0z European Ensemble Mean centered on Day 8, and 25%  
of Today's 0z Canadian Ensemble Mean centered on Day 8  
  
 
FORECAST CONFIDENCE FOR THE 6-10 DAY PERIOD: Above Normal, 4 out 5, Good  
agreement among the dynamical model, heights, temperatures, and precipitation  
forecast is offset slightly by concerns regarding lee-side cyclogenesis.  
 
8-14 DAY OUTLOOK FOR NOV 20 - 26 2026  
 
The week-2 ensemble forecasts from the GEFS, ECENS, and CMCE are again in fair  
agreement, particularly over the CONUS, for the forecast 7 day mean <a href="/products/predictions/610day/glossary.html#500-hPa">500-hPa</a>  
height pattern. Positive <a href="/products/predictions/610day/glossary.html#anomalies">anomalies</a> are again forecast across Canada and into  
the northeastern <a href="/products/predictions/610day/glossary.html#CONUS">CONUS</a>. The mid-level low pressure initially over the West  
Coast in the 6-10 day period, is undercutting this <a href="/products/predictions/610day/glossary.html#ridge">ridge</a> bringing below normal  
<a href="/products/predictions/610day/glossary.html#500-hPa">500-hPa</a> heights to much of the southern tier of the <a href="/products/predictions/610day/glossary.html#CONUS">CONUS</a>. In Alaska, there are  
differences with the GEFS favoring a much more robust <a href="/products/predictions/610day/glossary.html#ridge">ridge</a> further west than  
the ECENS and CMCE.  
 
Above normal temperatures remain forecast across most of the <a href="/products/predictions/610day/glossary.html#CONUS">CONUS</a> during  
week-2 as a result of mid-level high pressure across much of the eastern and  
northwestern <a href="/products/predictions/610day/glossary.html#CONUS">CONUS</a>. The highest probabilities are over the Great Lakes,  
Northeast, and Mid-Atlantic with probabilities for above normal temperatures  
exceeding 60%. Odds are reduced across portions of the Southeast and Gulf Coast  
with lower height <a href="/products/predictions/610day/glossary.html#anomalies">anomalies</a> and a mid-level <a href="/products/predictions/610day/glossary.html#trough">trough</a> progressing through the  
area. Over the West, near to below normal temperatures are favored over parts  
of the Great Basin and Four Corners region. Warming temperatures are favored  
across California and Oregon from the 6-10 day period with a slight tilt  
towards above normal favored. In Alaska, above normal temperatures are likely  
over most of the state with the highest degree of confidence over the eastern  
Mainland. Meanwhile, in Hawaii, above normal temperatures are favored for the  
Big Island with below normal temperatures favored for Oahu and Kauai.  
 
Above normal precipitation is favored from Oregon and California through much  
of the Rockies and into the Plains and Mississippi Valley associated with  
mid-level low pressure progressing across the southern tier and lee-side  
cyclogenesis. Probabilities are significantly reduced over the western <a href="/products/predictions/610day/glossary.html#CONUS">CONUS</a>  
relative to the 6-10 day period, with a period of dry weather favored to occur  
over the West Coast following early precipitation. Another period of enhanced  
wetness may occur by the end of week-2 over the West. A greater than 50% chance  
of above normal precipitation is forecast for the Central and Southern Plains.  
Near normal precipitation is favored for the northeastern <a href="/products/predictions/610day/glossary.html#CONUS">CONUS</a> consistent with  
most of the tools and elevated uncertainty regarding the path of any storm  
system leading into Thanksgiving. Below normal precipitation is favored for  
portions of Washington state closer to mid-level ridging. In Alaska, below  
normal precipitation is favored for Southeast Alaska, while near to above  
normal is generally favored over much of the Mainland. In Hawaii, below normal  
precipitation is favored for the island chain, consistent with consolidation  
tools and the winter <a href="/products/predictions/610day/glossary.html#El Nino">El Nino</a> signal. 
 
The official 8-14 day <a href="/products/predictions/610day/glossary.html#500-hPa">500-hPa</a> height <a href="/products/predictions/610day/glossary.html#blend">blend</a> consists of: 10% of Today's 0z <a href="/products/predictions/610day/glossary.html#GFS">GFS</a>  
Ensemble Mean centered on Day 11, 25% of Today's 6z <a href="/products/predictions/610day/glossary.html#GFS">GFS</a> Ensemble Mean centered  
on Day 11, 40% of Today's 0z European Ensemble Mean centered on Day 11, and 25%  
of Today's 0z Canadian Ensemble Mean centered on Day 11  
 
 
FORECAST CONFIDENCE FOR THE 8-14 DAY PERIOD: Above Normal, 4 out 5, Good  
agreement among the dynamical model, heights, temperatures, and precipitation  
forecast is offset slightly by concerns regarding lee-side cyclogenesis.  
 
FORECASTER: Ryan Bolt 
 
Notes: 
 
 
The notation for the categorical forecast indicated on the maps is  
the same as that in the tables: A-above   N-near normal   B-below  
  
The temperature map shows regions with > 33% chance of being warmer 
(orange, "A"), colder (blue, "B"), or close to (gray, "N") normal. 
Labels on the shaded lines give the probability (> 33%) of the more 
likely category (B or A). Probability of N is always < 40%. 
  
The precipitation map shows regions with > 33% chance of being  
wetter (green, "A"), drier (tan, "B"), or close to (gray, "N") 
normal. Labels on the shaded lines give the probability (> 33%) of 
the more likely category (B or A). Probability of N is always < 40%. 
  
In the southwest and other climatologically dry regions - there will  
be a greater than 33.3% chance of no precipitation and occasionally  
even a normal (i.e. Median) value of zero - especially during the dry  
seasons.&nbsp;&nbsp;In such cases a forecast of near normal is effectively a  
forecast of little or no precipitation.  
  
The climate prediction center uses 1991-2020 base period means as   
reference in the climate outlooks. 
 
The next set of long-lead monthly and seasonal outlooks will be released on  
November 19.
