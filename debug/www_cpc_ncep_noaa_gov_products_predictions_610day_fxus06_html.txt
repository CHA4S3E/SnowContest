Prognostic Discussion for 6 to 10 and 8 to 14 day outlooks 
NWS Climate Prediction Center College Park, MD 
300 PM EST Sat November 07 2026 
 
6-10 DAY OUTLOOK FOR NOV 13 - 17 2026 
 
The 0Z ECMWF, GEFS, and Canadian ensemble mean forecasts are in fairly good  
agreement on the average large-scale circulation pattern over the North America  
domain during the 6-10 day period. Today's manual height <a href="/products/predictions/610day/glossary.html#blend">blend</a> is based on  
these three model <a href="/products/predictions/610day/glossary.html#500-hPa">500-hPa</a> height forecasts, weighting the ECENS greater due to  
recent model skill. The manual <a href="/products/predictions/610day/glossary.html#blend">blend</a> indicates a deep slow-moving <a href="/products/predictions/610day/glossary.html#trough">trough</a> from  
the Bering Sea across the Aleutians and southwestern Alaska to the northeastern  
Pacific. This mid-level <a href="/products/predictions/610day/glossary.html#trough">trough</a> is accompanied by well below normal <a href="/products/predictions/610day/glossary.html#500-hPa">500-hPa</a>  
heights. Strong anomalous ridging with well above normal heights is forecast  
over much of central North America. Downstream, a mid-level <a href="/products/predictions/610day/glossary.html#trough">trough</a> is forecast  
just off the East Coast, with accompanying below normal heights. Over the  
subtropical Central Pacific, a flat anomalous <a href="/products/predictions/610day/glossary.html#ridge">ridge</a> is predicted which extends  
eastward across the Hawaiian Islands. 
  
Above normal temperatures are favored over the western and central CONUS, due  
to anomalous ridging ahead of the deep <a href="/products/predictions/610day/glossary.html#trough">trough</a> forecast over the northeastern  
Pacific. Maximum chances of above normal temperatures range from 80-90 percent  
over the northern Plains and neighboring parts of Nebraska and Minnesota,  
supported by most of the dynamical temperature forecast tools. Near to below  
normal temperatures are indicated over the eastern third of the <a href="/products/predictions/610day/glossary.html#CONUS">CONUS</a>. The  
eastern <a href="/products/predictions/610day/glossary.html#CONUS">CONUS</a> is forecast to be between the mid-continent <a href="/products/predictions/610day/glossary.html#ridge">ridge</a> axis and a  
<a href="/products/predictions/610day/glossary.html#trough">trough</a> off the Atlantic coast, with northeasterly anomalous flow. In Alaska,  
above normal temperatures are favored over the eastern half of the Mainland,  
and Southeast Alaska, with chances exceeding 50 percent over Southeast Alaska.  
This is broadly supported by the consolidation forecast, along with near normal  
temperatures for the western half of the Mainland. Well to the south, over the  
subtropical Central Pacific, temperatures over the Hawaiian Islands are favored  
to be above normal, in proximity to a mid-level <a href="/products/predictions/610day/glossary.html#ridge">ridge</a>. 
 
The positioning and strength of the cyclonic flow over the northeast Pacific  
Ocean promotes a stormy pattern across areas west of the Continental Divide,  
with above normal precipitation chances of 50-60 percent along most of the West  
Coast, and maximum odds of 70-80 percent south of San Francisco Bay. A weak  
<a href="/products/predictions/610day/glossary.html#southern stream">southern stream</a> disturbance ejecting from the base of the Pacific <a href="/products/predictions/610day/glossary.html#trough">trough</a> is  
expected to elevate precipitation chances even beyond the southern Continental  
Divide across much of the Southern Plains. Most of the remainder of the <a href="/products/predictions/610day/glossary.html#CONUS">CONUS</a>  
(with the exception of southern Florida) is favored to receive below to near  
normal precipitation amounts, largely due to the mid-continent <a href="/products/predictions/610day/glossary.html#ridge">ridge</a> and  
associated positive height <a href="/products/predictions/610day/glossary.html#anomalies">anomalies</a>. Maximum chances for below normal  
precipitation range from 40-50 percent across a large portion of the Central  
and interior Eastern <a href="/products/predictions/610day/glossary.html#CONUS">CONUS</a>. The mid-level <a href="/products/predictions/610day/glossary.html#trough">trough</a> predicted off the East Coast  
is expected to hang back across the Florida Peninsula, promoting above normal  
precipitation. At higher latitudes, above normal precipitation is favored for  
the Aleutians, Mainland Alaska, and Southeast Alaska, under a mid-level <a href="/products/predictions/610day/glossary.html#trough">trough</a>  
predicted over the Bering Sea. A slight tilt in the odds for drier than normal  
conditions is favored over the Hawaiian Islands, consistent with the ERF-CON  
precipitation tool.  
 
The official 6-10 day <a href="/products/predictions/610day/glossary.html#500-hPa">500-hPa</a> height <a href="/products/predictions/610day/glossary.html#blend">blend</a> consists of 30% of Today's 0z <a href="/products/predictions/610day/glossary.html#GFS">GFS</a>  
Ensemble Mean centered on Day 8, 40% of Today's 0z European Ensemble Mean  
centered on Day 8, and 30% of Today's 0z Canadian Ensemble Mean centered on Day  
8  
  
 
FORECAST CONFIDENCE FOR THE 6-10 DAY PERIOD: Above average, 4 out of 5, due to  
improved agreement (relative to yesterday) among the various model height  
forecasts, and good agreement among the surface temperature and precipitation  
tools. 
 
 
8-14 DAY OUTLOOK FOR NOV 15 - 21 2026  
 
The 0Z ECMWF, GEFS, and Canadian ensemble mean solutions for the 8-14 day  
period predict a fairly consistent <a href="/products/predictions/610day/glossary.html#500-hPa">500-hPa</a> circulation pattern relative to the  
6-10 day period across North America and the surrounding regions, though with  
diminished amplitude. The strong anomalous <a href="/products/predictions/610day/glossary.html#ridge">ridge</a> over central North America is  
predicted to expand across most of Canada, and over the Central and interior  
Eastern Contiguous U.S. (CONUS). The northwestern portion of this <a href="/products/predictions/610day/glossary.html#ridge">ridge</a> is  
forecast to overspread parts of northeastern Alaska. The more aggressive GEFS  
and ECENS solutions displace the storm track (and associated mid-level trough)  
southward across the Aleutians and the northeastern Pacific. The eastern extent  
of this <a href="/products/predictions/610day/glossary.html#trough">trough</a> shifts across much of the western CONUS, with the Pacific jet  
stream being shunted southward as well along the West Coast. The mean <a href="/products/predictions/610day/glossary.html#trough">trough</a>  
off the East Coast is predicted by the three model ensemble solutions to  
advance well to the east, with the largest negative height departures now well  
to the southeast of Newfoundland. Over the subtropical Central Pacific, the  
GEFS and ECENS solutions rapidly dismantle the <a href="/products/predictions/610day/glossary.html#ridge">ridge</a> depicted there in the  
earlier 6-10 day period, with the CMCE still retaining a significant, but  
weakened anomalous <a href="/products/predictions/610day/glossary.html#ridge">ridge</a>. 
 
Above normal temperatures are favored across most of the <a href="/products/predictions/610day/glossary.html#CONUS">CONUS</a> associated with  
anomalous ridging and positive height <a href="/products/predictions/610day/glossary.html#anomalies">anomalies</a> ahead of the deep <a href="/products/predictions/610day/glossary.html#trough">trough</a> over  
the northeastern Pacific. The highest confidence (greater than 80 percent  
chance) for anomalous warmth is indicated for parts of the Northern Plains, the  
Upper Mississippi Valley, and portions of the upper Great Lakes. This nearly  
coast-to-coast warmth is supported by most of the dynamical temperature tools.  
The exception is over parts of southern Florida, where near normal temperatures  
are favored due to the residual effects of the offshore <a href="/products/predictions/610day/glossary.html#trough">trough</a> as it moves  
farther out over the Atlantic. Above normal temperatures are forecasted over  
the Aleutians, Mainland Alaska, and Southeast Alaska, under southeasterly flow.  
 Over the Central Pacific, despite the forecast of a weakening subtropical  
ridge, above normal temperature chances remain elevated for Hawaii, in large  
part driven by the relatively warm sea-surface temperatures (SSTs) associated  
with the current <a href="/products/predictions/610day/glossary.html#El Nino">El Nino</a>. 
 
Above normal precipitation is expected to overspread the western and central  
<a href="/products/predictions/610day/glossary.html#CONUS">CONUS</a> as far east as the Mississippi River, and continue eastward across  
southern Florida. This is due to the northeastern Pacific <a href="/products/predictions/610day/glossary.html#trough">trough</a> pushing  
farther inland across the West, meeting up with moist, low-level return flow  
circulating around surface high pressure over the Southeast. Maximum chances  
for above normal precipitation range from 50-60 percent over parts of the  
southwestern and south-central <a href="/products/predictions/610day/glossary.html#CONUS">CONUS</a>. Below normal precipitation amounts are  
favored for most of the Great Lakes region, the upper Ohio and Tennessee  
Valleys, most of the Appalachians, and the Atlantic coastal plain as far south  
as the Carolinas. This region lies in the subsidence area between the  
mid-continent <a href="/products/predictions/610day/glossary.html#ridge">ridge</a> axis and the offshore <a href="/products/predictions/610day/glossary.html#trough">trough</a>. In Alaska, above normal  
precipitation is favored over the Aleutians, Mainland Alaska, and Southeast  
Alaska, under a <a href="/products/predictions/610day/glossary.html#trough">trough</a> predicted over the Bering Sea. Across the subtropical  
Central Pacific, the mid-level <a href="/products/predictions/610day/glossary.html#ridge">ridge</a> is forecast to break down, allowing more  
moisture to reach the Hawaiian Islands. Near normal precipitation is favored  
for the Hawaiian Islands, consistent with an automated <a href="/products/predictions/610day/glossary.html#blend">blend</a>.   
 
The official 8-14 day <a href="/products/predictions/610day/glossary.html#500-hPa">500-hPa</a> height <a href="/products/predictions/610day/glossary.html#blend">blend</a> consists of: 30% of Today's 0z <a href="/products/predictions/610day/glossary.html#GFS">GFS</a>  
Ensemble Mean centered on Day 11, 40% of Today's 0z European Ensemble Mean  
centered on Day 11, and 30% of Today's 0z Canadian Ensemble Mean centered on  
Day 11  
 
 
FORECAST CONFIDENCE FOR THE 8-14 DAY PERIOD: Near average, 3 out of 5, due to  
reasonable agreement among today's model height solutions, offset by some  
differences among model temperature and precipitation tools.   
 
FORECASTER: Luke H 
 
Notes: 
 
 
The notation for the categorical forecast indicated on the maps is  
the same as that in the tables: A-above   N-near normal   B-below  
  
The temperature map shows regions with > 33% chance of being warmer 
(orange, "A"), colder (blue, "B"), or close to (gray, "N") normal. 
Labels on the shaded lines give the probability (> 33%) of the more 
likely category (B or A). Probability of N is always < 40%. 
  
The precipitation map shows regions with > 33% chance of being  
wetter (green, "A"), drier (tan, "B"), or close to (gray, "N") 
normal. Labels on the shaded lines give the probability (> 33%) of 
the more likely category (B or A). Probability of N is always < 40%. 
  
In the southwest and other climatologically dry regions - there will  
be a greater than 33.3% chance of no precipitation and occasionally  
even a normal (i.e. Median) value of zero - especially during the dry  
seasons.&nbsp;&nbsp;In such cases a forecast of near normal is effectively a  
forecast of little or no precipitation.  
  
The climate prediction center uses 1991-2020 base period means as   
reference in the climate outlooks. 
 
The next set of long-lead monthly and seasonal outlooks will be released on  
November 19.
