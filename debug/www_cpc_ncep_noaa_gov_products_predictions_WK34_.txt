										<FONT FACE="Courier"><div align="left"><strong>
										Prognostic Discussion for Week 3-4 Temperature and Precipitation Outlooks
<br>NWS Climate Prediction Center College Park MD
<br>300PM EST Fri Nov 06 2026
<br>
<br>Week 3-4 Forecast Discussion Valid Sat Nov 21 2026-Fri Dec 04 2026
<br>
<br>Various tropical and extratropical signals currently at play may influence the pattern across the US during the Weeks 3-4 period. The ongoing El Niño event remains the dominant driver of global tropical convective anomalies, and the robust atmospheric response to above-average SSTs across the equatorial central and eastern Pacific is the most prominent contributor to the statistical guidance, including the multivariate linear regression (MLR) model. The Madden-Julian Oscillation (MJO) is also active, with its enhanced convective phase currently over the Indian Ocean basin, where it is interacting with a developing positive phase of the Indian Ocean Dipole (IOD). The MJO is unlikely to drive any significant extratropical response, as the ENSO footprint remains much stronger. Additionally, an unusually early sudden stratospheric warming (SSW) event is underway over the Arctic region, with a weakening polar vortex potentially associated with a negative phase of the Arctic Oscillation (AO) and North Atlantic Oscillation (NAO) during the Week-1 period. Dynamical model forecasts show this warming event to be relatively short-lived, however, and GEFS ensemble forecasts of the AO show the negative phase weakening by the end of Week-2. Despite these numerous conflicting signals, the ENSO response is favored to be the primary influencer of the Weeks 3-4 pattern during the outlook period.
<br>
<br>A manual blend of dynamical and statistical model guidance results in a forecast height anomaly pattern strongly consistent with a canonical El Niño response. Strong troughing south of the Aleutians may promote enhanced southerly flow over southern Alaska, and onshore flow of Pacific air into the western CONUS. Below-average 500-hPa height anomalies extend across the southern tier of the CONUS, with a pronounced ridge over central and eastern Canada, extending across the Great Lakes and Northeast. Despite the SSW event, this pattern limits the availability of Arctic air masses across North America that could drive any significant cold air outbreaks into the US during the Weeks 3-4 period, while increasing the availability of moisture in the atmosphere across the southern tier.
<br>
<br>The Weeks 3-4 temperature outlook indicates enhanced chances for above-normal temperatures across Alaska, consistent with mean southerly flow. Above-normal temperatures are also favored across the West Coast states, partly due to above-normal SSTs along the coast, and across the northern tier consistent with the forecast height anomaly pattern. The highest probabilities for above-normal temperatures extend across the Great Lakes region and the Northeast. While above-normal temperatures are slightly favored across the Southeast, dynamical models indicate a potential for colder temperatures extending into the region during Week-4. In contrast, below-normal temperatures are favored for the Four Corners region and much of Texas, consistent with the below-normal 500-hPa height anomalies. Above-normal temperatures are favored across Hawaii, under enhanced subtropical ridging, which is typical during El Niño events.
<br>
<br>The Weeks 3-4 precipitation outlook favors above-average precipitation for southeastern Alaska and the West Coast, consistent with enhanced onshore flow. Many dynamical models show a stronger enhanced signal across the Northwest, while statistical guidance, largely influenced by El Niño, shows higher probabilities across the Southwest. Given the strong ENSO signal and a lower climatological threshold across the Southwest, the highest probabilities for above-normal precipitation extend across much of California. Above-normal precipitation is favored across the southern half of the CONUS, with higher probabilities across the Southeast and Eastern Seaboard northward into southern New England, where an enhanced storm track is anticipated. Below-normal precipitation is forecast for the northern Rockies and northern Plains, as well as Hawaii, due to anticipated ridging.
<br>
<br>Forecaster: Adam Allgood
<br>
<br>The next week 3-4 outlook will be issued on Friday, Nov 13, 2026
<br>
<br>These outlooks are based on departures from the 1991-2020 base period
<br>
<br>These are three category outlooks consistent with what is currently used for the monthly and seasonal forecasts.
<br>
<br><br><br>The shading on the temperature map depicts the most favored category, either above-normal (A), below-normal (B), or near-normal (N) with the solid lines giving the probability ( >33%) of this more likely category (above, below, or near).
<br>
<br>The shading on the precipitation map depicts the most favored category, either above-median (A), below-median (B), or near-median with the solid lines giving the probability ( >33%) of this more likely category (above, below, or near).
<br>
<br>In areas where the likelihoods of 2-week mean temperatures and accumulated precipitation amounts are similar to climatological probabilities, equal chances (EC) is indicated.
<br>
