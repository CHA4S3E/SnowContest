          <h2>Valid Friday November 13, 2026 to Thursday November 19, 2026</h2>US Hazards Outlook<br>NWS Climate Prediction Center College Park MD<br>300 PM 
EST November 05 2026<br><br><u>Synopsis</u>: Dynamical models continue to 
depict mid-level low pressure near the West Coast of the Contiguous U.S. 
(CONUS) early in week-2. However, the amplitude of this feature has decreased 
compared to yesterday with decreasing precipitation amounts in much of the 
guidance, particularly later in the period. Given that a potent surge of 
moisture is still likely at the start of the period, elevated risks of heavy 
precipitation, high elevation heavy snow, and coastal high winds remain across 
the Pacific Northwest and into northern and central California. Southern Stream 
energy may also propagate into the Southwest and Four Corners leading to 
enhanced precipitation across areas recently impacted by heavy rainfall and 
flooding. Above-normal temperatures are favored for much of the CONUS, although 
areas of the Middle Mississippi Valley, Ohio Valley, and Mid-Atlantic may 
experience near freezing overnight temperatures during the period, which would

Thursday November 19: </a> The 0z ECMWF, GEFS, and Canadian ensembles continue 
to depict anomalous troughing off the West Coast of the CONUS. However, 
compared to yesterday, the amplitude of the trough is weaker. While an influx 
of moisture is likely late in week-1 into early week-2, the strongest signals 
in the Integrated Vapor Transport (IVT) tools derived from the GEFS and ECMWF 
ensembles are mainly prior to day-8, and centered farther north along the coast 
of British Columbia. Around the start of week-2, IVT signals increase farther 
south, consistent with enhanced precipitation amounts across the Pacific 
Northwest into northern and central California. However, probabilities for 
3-day precipitation totals reaching 2 inches have dropped significantly in the 
uncalibrated ECMWF and GEFS ensembles. In addition, the Probabilistic Extremes 
Tools (PETs) only indicate a 20-40 percent chance 3-day precipitation totals 
exceed the 85th climatological percentile and 1-2 inches. The moderate risk of 
heavy precipitation remains in place Nov 13-15 given the recent volatility in 
the model guidance, but if drying trends continue a downgrade to a slight risk 
is likely in tomorrow's update. Due to the weaker precipitation signals and 
many areas of the Pacific Northwest having below-normal rainfall for the past 
30-days, the flooding possible hazard is removed. Moderate risks for coastal 
high winds and high elevation heavy snow for the Klamath and Sierra Nevadas 
remain in place for Nov 13-15, but these are also more marginal with a 
reduction in probabilities for 3-day snow amounts greater than 6-inches, with 
snow levels expected to be high. Slight risks of heavy precipitation and snow 
surround the moderate risks but are now only valid through day-12 (Nov 17) as 
the trough weakens further and propagates inland across the CONUS, with the 
PETs losing the signal for enhanced precipitation across the Pacific Northwest 
late in week-2. <br><br>There is a southward extension in the GEFS probabilities for IVT to exceed 
250 kg/ms at the outset of the period, with some evidence of another extension 
around the middle of the period in the ECMWF ensemble. In addition, the PETs 
depict stronger probabilities in percentile space (greater than 40 percent) 
across portions of the Southwest and Four Corners. Much of this is due to the 
lower precipitation climatology. However, both the uncalibrated and calibrated 
ECMWF indicate 3-day probabilities of at least 20 percent for 3-day 
precipitation to exceed 1-inch in the middle of week-2. The GEFS has also 
shifted relatively higher probabilities over the region compared to yesterday 
despite the lower precipitation amounts farther north. As a result, the slight 
risk of heavy precipitation is extended over this area, with any additional 
precipitation over already saturated ground resulting in flooding concerns.<br><br>Across the remainder of the CONUS, ridging and above-normal temperatures 
are more likely, with troughing predicted near the East Coast early in the 
period. There is some potential for southern stream shortwave energy near the 
Gulf Coast and Southeast early in the period, which may lead to some 
precipitation enhancement. The ECMWF and Canadian ensembles depict surface low 
pressure off the northeastern coast at the outset of the period. While coastal 
wind and precipitation would be a concern, the GEFS does not show this feature 
leading to more uncertainty. Despite the above-normal temperatures there is 
some concern for overnight temperatures near freezing across portions of the 
Middle Mississippi Valley, Ohio Valley, and Mid-Atlantic, which for many of 
these areas would mark the first freeze, 2-3 weeks behind climatology. Both the 
GEFS and ECMWF PETs depict at least a 20 percent chance temperatures fall below 
32 deg F as surface high pressure settles into the region. While no hazard is 
posted today, particularly given the surface pattern across the East at the 
start of the period, some consideration may be needed in future updates given 
potential impacts to vegetation that would be susceptible to a first freeze.<br><br>Tied to surface high pressure shifting into the eastern CONUS and troughing 
initially near the West Coast moving farther inland, enhanced southerly flow 
from the Gulf is possible across the Southern Plains and adjacent Mississippi 
Valley. The GEFS and Canadian are more amplified with the trough resulting in a 
stronger precipitation response late in week-2 compared to the ECMWF ensemble. 
However, PETs and uncalibrated models do not support a corresponding 
precipitation risk at this time, but this potential will continue to be 
monitored in upcoming outlooks. <br><br>While much of the strong troughing over the northeastern Pacific is likely 
to result in periods of strong winds and heavy precipitation along the southern 
and southeastern coast of Alaska as well, hazardous thresholds are not expected 
to be exceeded so no hazards are posted for Alaska at this time.
