          <h2>Valid Monday November 16, 2026 to Sunday November 22, 2026</h2>US Hazards Outlook<br>NWS Climate Prediction Center College Park MD<br>300 PM 
EST November 08 2026<br><br><u>Synopsis</u>: An area of mid-level low pressure 
is favored to move across the southern Contiguous U.S. (CONUS) during the 
week-2 forecast period, bringing the potential for a variety of hazardous 
weather from the Desert Southwest across portions of the Rocky Mountains and 
into the Great Plains and Mississippi Valley. Persistent mid-level low pressure 
over the North Pacific may tap into enhanced atmospheric moisture, resuming 
atmospheric river activity over the Pacific Northwest after a brief respite 
early in the forecast period. <br><br><u>Hazards</u> <ul><li>Slight risk of 
heavy precipitation for portions of Southern California and the Desert 
Southwest, Mon-Tue, Nov 16-17.<li>Slight risk of heavy precipitation for portions of the Southern and Central 
Plains and the Lower and Middle Mississippi Valley, Wed-Fri, Nov 18-20.<li>Slight risk of heavy precipitation for western portions of the Pacific

href='/products/predictions/814day/index.php'> For Monday November 16 - Sunday 
November 22: </a> Multiple model ensembles indicate some reorganization of 
persistent mid-level troughing over the North Pacific as a shortwave trough is 
favored to eject out of the base of the larger trough at the outset of the 
week-2 period. Strong ridging is predicted downstream resulting in a relatively 
slow earward propagation of the trough along the Southern Tier. As this feature 
moves eastward models indicate the potential for a variety of hazardous weather 
from the Desert Southwest to the Mississippi Valley. Model ensembles also favor 
a reloading of troughing over the North Pacific, potentially bringing renewed 
atmospheric river activity for much of the Pacific Northwest by mid week-2.<br><br>Hazardous weather associated with the shortwave trough begins over the 
Desert Southwest at the outset of the forecast period. The Probabilistic 
Extremes Tools (PETs) from the ECMWF and GEFS both indicate at least a 20% 
chance of 3-day precipitation accumulation to exceed the 85th climatological 
percentile and at least half and inch over much of Southern California and the 
lower Four Corners, with uncalibrated model probabilities exceeding similar 
thresholds. The ECMWF is particularly bullish, indicating similar probabilities 
for exceeding one inch of accumulated precipitation. Even discounting this 
aggressive solution there is sufficient support to warrant a slight risk of 
heavy precipitation for much of Southern California and the Desert Southwest 
valid Nov 16-17. Localized urban and flash flooding is possible due to 
antecedent precipitation, although no associated flooding hazard is issued at 
this time.<br><br>As the mid-level trough and associated moisture move inland, enhanced snow 
is anticipated over much of the Intermountain West. This is well indicated by 
the GEFS PET, which shows a 20% chance of three-day snow water equivalent to 
exceed the 85th climatological percentile through the middle of week-2, and 
uncalibrated probabilities from the ECMWF and GEFS indicate at least a 20% 
chance of 3-day accumulated snowfall to exceed 6 inches for much of the 
forecast period. Therefore a slight risk of heavy snow is posted for much of 
the Northern and Central Rockies and Wasatch Range for Nov 16-20.<br><br>As the mid-level trough continues east a round of surface low cyclogenesis 
is indicated by the middle of week-2, which is favored to tap into Gulf 
moisture resulting in enhanced precipitation ahead of the surface low. The GEFS 
and ECMWF are initially in good agreement with the onset of heavy precipitation 
on day-10 (Nov 18) over the Southern Plains and Mississippi Valley, although 
their solutions diverge quickly with regard to the intensity and extent of the 
heaviest precipitation. Based on best consensus a slight risk of heavy 
precipitation is posted for eastern portions of the Southern and Central 
Plains, and the Lower and Middle Mississippi Valley where probabilities of 
3-day precipitation accumulation to exceed 1 inch from both models best align, 
valid Nov 18-20. More aggressive model solutions would argue for longer 
duration and possibly a moderate risk to be issued as well, but lack of better 
support from guidance precludes these scenarios. This situation will be closely 
monitored in the coming days. As the surface low develops models indicate tight 
surface pressure gradients and accompanying enhanced winds over the Great 
Plains, therefore a slight risk of high winds is indicated for much of the 
Great Plains for Nov 16-18.<br><br>Persistent mid-level troughing over the North Pacific is favored to weaken 
slightly as the shortwave trough exits the larger tough axis early in week-2, 
but multiple model ensembles depict a reloading of this longwave trough as 
week-2 progresses, potentially resulting in a resumption of atmospheric river 
activity over the Pacific Northwest for much of the forecast period after a 
brief lull at the outset of week-2. Uncalibrated probabilities from the GEFS 
and ECMWF both indicate at least a 20% chance of 3-day precipitation 
accumulations to exceed 1.5 inches across much of the Pacific Northwest from 
the middle of week-2 onward. Similar probability thresholds are met for 
snowfall accumulations to exceed 6 inches at higher elevations along coastal 
ranges and the northern Sierra Nevadas. Integrated water vapor flux forecasts 
are not particularly impressive over the same period, but nonetheless there is 
sufficient support to issue a slight risk of heavy precipitation for much of 
the western Pacific Northwest valid Nov 18-22, along with a slight risk of 
heavy snow for the Cascades, Klamath, and northern Sierra Nevada mountains, and 
a slight risk of high wind along the Pacific Northwest coast, both also valid 
for Nov 18-22.<br><br>While much of the strong troughing over the northeastern Pacific and Bering 
Sea is likely to result in periods of strong winds and heavy precipitation 
along the southern and southeastern coast of Alaska, hazardous thresholds are 
not expected to be exceeded, so no hazards are posted for Alaska at this
