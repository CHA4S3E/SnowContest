.DISCUSSION...
KEY MESSAGE 1: Unseasonably mild through Friday with 2.5 weeks-
long dry spell now over.

A slow moving and weak cold front over Eastern Pennsylvania will 
be the focus for some morning showers across the region to the 
East of the Route 15/Interstate 81 corridors. A secondary cold 
front will push through Northwestern PA later this morning and 
move across the rest of Central PA and the Susquehanna Valley this
afternoon. 

One more day of unseasonably warm temperatures across much of
the CWA, though even highs in the mid to upper 50s across the NW
Mtns is still quite mild for early Nov.

High temp departures from normal today will be +10 to 12F 
across much of the Northern and Western Mtns and as much as +15F
above normal throughout the Central and Southern Valleys. 


KEY MESSAGE 2: Trending cooler this weekend with lake enhanced 
rain showers beneath upper level low

Update...
00Z guidance continues to highlight the trough of the upcoming 
cooldown occurring Sat morning when a pocket of -7 to -8C 850 
mb temps pivots through NRN PA accompanied by deep moisture and 
some mesoscale focus for lift and occasional rain/wet snow 
showers along and in the wake of yet a 3rd cfrontal boundary 
with a boost of enhanced orographic lift. 

Its not out of the question to see a light slushy coating of
snow during the mid/late morning hours Sat at elevations above
2100 ft MSL across the NW and over 2600 MSL in the Laurel
Highlands - the first of this mild/very pleasant fall season so
far. GFS is by far the coldest of the model guidance but it 
should be highlighted than Lake Erie is still 60-62F (near or
slightly over record warm water temps for this date). This will
undoubtedly temper this cooler guidance. 

Previous disc... 

We have been constantly been pulling NBM (mean) temp guidance 
down closer to MOS blend for Sat, and we've now done it more so 
with continued support from many sources. We have also pulled 
temps up a few degs for Sat night due to the same clouds and 
also wind which should keep us more- mixed. 

Seasonably cooler/breezy pattern to start the first full 
weekend of November. The passage of closed H5 low followed by 
mean cyclonic flow favors persistent clouds and periods of lake 
enhanced rain over the western and central Alleghenies on 
Saturday. We were keen to manually adjust max temps a few 
degrees lower on 11/7 to account for the cooler pattern shift 
which looks to continue into early next week. If the current 
forecast holds, it will be the first below average daytime high 
in three weeks!

.AVIATION /12Z FRIDAY THROUGH TUESDAY/...
Patchy MVFR ceilings (KMDT/KLNS) and localized fog (mainly 
KIPT, with patchy BR/FG around KMDT/KLNS as well) will persist 
over eastern Pennsylvania this morning as a cold front slowly 
presses eastward. A compact shortwave moving over the Great 
Lakes will reinvigorate convection ahead of the front this 
morning, mainly along the I-95 corridor, though a few showers 
may move across KLNS before drier air moves in for the 
afternoon. While thunder cannot be ruled out at KLNS (10% 
chance), MUCAPE trending towards 0 J/kg around the onset of 
these showers precludes thunder mentions at this time. For KMDT,
drier air filtering in behind the front will limit shower 
activity, with PoPs of around 10-20%. Brief MVFR visibilities 
will be possible with these showers, primarily at KLNS, with 
restrictions otherwise unlikely.

Otherwise, surface winds today will continue to trend westerly 
behind the front, with cloud bases lowering as the shortwave 
approaches from the northwest. Ceiling restrictions will be most
likely at KBFD/KJST within this common upslope/lake flow setup,
primarily around and after 18Z Friday, with lower chances east 
of the Alleghenies. A few light showers will be possible as 
well, mainly over KBFD closer to the center of the shortwave. 
Additionally, brief periods of VFR may occur at KBFD/KJST 
between roughly 00Z and 06Z Saturday as a dry slot wraps around 
the center of the shortwave before low-level moisture flows 
back in.

Outlook...

Sat-Mon...Breezy with showers and sub-VFR ceilings possible 
over the NW 1/2 of the airspace. Max gusts 20-25kt.

Tue...Mainly VFR with westerly winds gusting up to around 20-25
kts during the daytime.
